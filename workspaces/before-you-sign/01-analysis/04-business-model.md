# Business Model & Revenue Strategy

## The core tension to resolve first
The product's entire value is **being unconflicted**. That single fact rules out the
most obvious fintech revenue sources and *defines* which models are viable:
- ❌ **Commissions / lead-gen to a named product/issuer** — destroys neutrality, and
  edges toward regulated "arranging/advising." **Do not do this.** It is the easy
  money that would kill the company's reason to exist.
- ❌ **Selling/recommending a "better" product** — turns you into a robo-adviser
  (licensing) and re-introduces the conflict.
- ✅ Revenue must come from **people/organisations who benefit from the buyer being
  better-informed**: the buyer, their employer, or a fee-only adviser.

## Recommended model: **B2B2C-led, with a B2C freemium funnel**
The pitch's 5-tier consumer ladder is fine as a *funnel*, but pure B2C consumer
fintech has brutal CAC and the purchase is *low-frequency* (you buy an ILP rarely),
so willingness to pay for a one-off report is weak. The durable economics are B2B2C.
Run both; lead with B2B2C for revenue, use B2C for distribution, data, and credibility.

### Revenue stream 1 (primary): **Employer / financial-wellness B2B**
Sell seats to employers as a **financial-wellness benefit**. Staff get "Before You
Sign" to sanity-check any product they're pitched. Employers already buy wellness
benefits; this is concrete and high-trust.
- **Pricing:** per-employee-per-year (e.g. S$X/employee/yr) or tiered platform fee.
- **Why it works:** recurring (SaaS), one sale = thousands of users (low CAC), and
  the unconflicted angle is exactly what an employer can endorse without liability.

### Revenue stream 2 (primary): **Fee-only / independent advisers & "trust" partners**
License the tool to **fee-based (non-commission) advisers, MAS-licensed planners,
and financial-coaching firms** as a client-facing transparency artefact — it makes
*their* fee-only pitch credible ("we'll even show you the cost of what others sell
you").
- **Pricing:** per-seat SaaS / white-label licence.
- **Synergy:** these partners are *unconflicted like us*, so no values clash.

### Revenue stream 3 (secondary): **B2C freemium (the pitch's ladder, tightened)**
- **Free:** basic scan — product summary, guarantee check, count of concern areas
  (the pitch's tier 2). Acquisition + data.
- **Paid one-off full report** (tier 3): fee breakdown in dollars, downside
  simulation, contextual fit, questions to ask. Price as a **cheap insurance against
  a big mistake** ("S$__ to understand a S$100k decision").
- **Premium subscription** (tier 4): multiple reports, saved reviews, Portfolio
  Mirror, alerts. Monthly/annual.
- **Family access** (tier 5): household plan — strong, because the *decision-maker*
  who reviews parents' products is a high-intent payer.

### Revenue stream 4 (later / institutional): **Anonymised, aggregated insight**
Once enough PHS documents flow through, an **anonymised benchmark dataset** of
typical fees/terms per product class has value to *non-conflicted* buyers: consumer
associations, media, academics, even regulators (MoneySense). **Never** sell
user-level data; never sell to the issuers being analysed. Treat with care (PDPA,
reputation). This is upside, not the core plan.

### Channel / grant angle: **MoneySense / public-education partnership**
Not direct revenue, but a credibility-and-distribution multiplier: alignment with
the national financial-literacy agenda lowers CAC and strengthens the "unconflicted"
brand. Worth a slide as go-to-market.

## Unit economics — the drivers to show (this is what the brief wants)
The proposal must articulate *the levers*, even with assumed numbers:

| Lever | Why it matters | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| **CAC** | B2C fintech CAC is high; B2B2C amortises one sale over many users | B2B2C ↓↓ |
| **Marginal cost per report** | Dominated by **LLM extraction cost** per document | Engineer to keep <S$ per report; deterministic math is ~free |
| **Conversion free→paid** | Low-frequency purchase weakens B2C conversion | Use B2B2C to bypass |
| **Retention / frequency** | People buy products rarely → churn risk in B2C subscription | Premium/Family + employer renewals fix this |
| **Gross margin** | SaaS-like once content (rubric/checklist) is built | High at scale |
| **Content build cost** | The rubric/checklist/question-bank is upfront IP | One-time, amortised |

**Key economic insight to state plainly:** the product is **high-stakes,
low-frequency** for any one consumer — which is *fatal* for pure B2C subscription
but *ideal* for B2B2C (the employer/adviser provides the always-on access; the
consumer dips in only when they're about to sign).

## Recommendation for the proposal
Lead the business-model slide with **B2B2C (employer + fee-only adviser) as the
revenue engine**, position the **consumer freemium ladder as the funnel and brand**,
name the **anonymised-benchmark data** as future upside, and **explicitly reject
commission/lead-gen** — and explain *why that rejection is the moat*. That framing is
more sophisticated than the pitch's 5-tier B2C ladder and directly answers
"soundness of the business model" + "key drivers of its economics."
