# Unit Economics & Revenue Model (resolves D1–D4) — Consumer Freemium-First

Go-to-market decision: **consumer freemium-first**, with the **free-look wedge** (see
`09-strategic-resolutions.md` A1) as the conversion engine, and B2B2C as the
*expansion* story (not the lead). All numbers are illustrative and assumption-stated —
the point is to show the *drivers*, which is exactly what the brief asks for.

## Pricing architecture (tightened from the pitch's 5 tiers)
| Tier | Price (illustrative) | What it unlocks | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Free Quick Scan** | S$0 | Term flags, guarantee check, # concerns, "questions to ask" sheet | Acquisition + data seed |
| **Full Report** (one-off) | **S$19** | Fee Lens (dollar drag), downside sim, RiskFit context, decision-gap | **Free-look conversion** |
| **Premium** (subscription) | **S$7/mo or S$60/yr** | Unlimited reports, saved reviews, Portfolio Mirror, alerts | Retention |
| **Family** | **S$108/yr** | Household profiles (parents/spouse) | High-intent ARPU lift |

Framing that drives willingness-to-pay: *"S$19 to understand a S$100,000 decision —
and you can still cancel free this week."* The cost of being wrong (≈S$100k fee gap,
per the worked example) dwarfs the price by ~5,000×.

## The economic problem with consumer-first — and how the wedge fixes it
- **Problem:** buying an ILP is **low-frequency** (once every few years) → weak
  subscription retention; and CAC is high in consumer fintech.
- **Fix 1 (frequency):** the free-look wedge creates a *recurring, deadline-driven*
  reason to return (every new product, every family member's product, every legacy
  "should I divorce my ILP?" review). MoneyOwl's "divorce my ILP" content shows this
  demand exists.
- **Fix 2 (CAC):** the free-look deadline is inherently **viral and shareable**
  ("check yours before day 14"); content/SEO around ILP fees is cheap to own; this is
  organic-led, not paid-led.

## Illustrative unit economics (state every assumption)
**Funnel (Year 1, conservative):**
```
Reachable ILP/unit-trust buyers + recent buyers (SOM, yr1)   ~120,000
→ Free Quick Scan users (8% reach)                              ~9,600
→ Full Report conversion (4% of free)                             ~384  × S$19  = S$7,296
→ Premium subscribers (1.5% of free)                              ~144  × S$60  = S$8,640
→ Family (0.3% of free)                                             ~29  × S$108 = S$3,132
Year-1 illustrative revenue (pre-scale)                                    ≈ S$19,000
```
This is intentionally small — it's a *student-credible* year-1, not a hockey stick.
The story is the **drivers and the slope**, not year-1 size.

**Cost & margin drivers:**
| Driver | Assumption | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Marginal cost / full report | ~S$0.20–0.50 | LLM extraction only; deterministic math ≈ free |
| Gross margin | **~85–90%** | SaaS-like once rubric/checklist content is built |
| CAC (blended) | S$3–12 | Organic/SEO + free-look virality keep it low |
| Content build (one-off) | the rubric/checklist/benchmark | Upfront IP, amortised |
| LTV (Premium) | price × retention; modest | Boosted by family + review cadence |
| **LTV / CAC target** | **> 3×** | Achievable *because* CAC is organic-led |

## Scaling levers (what an investor would underwrite)
1. **Conversion %** — the free-look deadline is the single biggest lever; A/B the
   deadline-urgency UX.
2. **Reach** — SEO/content ownership of "ILP fees / should I buy this" queries.
3. **Frequency** — expand product types (unit trusts → structured notes) and family
   profiles to lift reviews-per-user.
4. **ARPU** — Premium + Family tiers; later, benchmark-data products.

## Future / expansion revenue (mention, don't lead)
- **B2B2C** — fee-only advisers (trust artefact) + employer wellness benefit. Higher-
  margin, recurring, low CAC; the natural Series-A expansion once the consumer brand
  and benchmark data exist.
- **Anonymised, aggregated fee benchmarks** — sold only to *non-conflicted* buyers
  (media, associations, academics); never user-level, never to analysed issuers
  (D3 neutrality rule).

## Neutrality guardrail (D3 — non-negotiable)
No commissions, no lead-gen to a named product, no selling user data, no selling to
the issuers we analyse. Neutrality is the entire moat; the revenue model is
*designed around* protecting it. This is a feature to state proudly, not a constraint.

## Go-to-market sequence (D4)
**Phase 1 (MVP/now):** consumer free-look tool for ILPs; own the "ILP fees" content.
**Phase 2:** Premium + Family; expand to unit trusts; grow seeded benchmark corpus.
**Phase 3:** fee-only adviser partnerships (B2B2C); employer wellness pilots.
**Phase 4:** anonymised benchmark-data product. Each phase reuses the same engine.
