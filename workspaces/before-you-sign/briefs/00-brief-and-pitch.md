# Brief & Source Pitch — "Before You Sign"

## Course
FNCE6041 — Financial Analytics and Innovation (SMU MBA, May Term). Group assignment.

## The Assignment Brief (verbatim intent)
Each group develops a fintech idea into a **well-structured, investment-quality proposal**. Required elements:

1. **Problem** — clear articulation of the problem solved, target users, market context.
2. **Product design** — how the solution works and the user experience.
3. **Business model** — how the product generates revenue and the key drivers of its economics.
4. **Financial/analytical component (course core)** — a pricing framework, risk model, portfolio construction logic, credit assessment approach, or a simple backtest/simulation. Must demonstrate rigorous reasoning and quantitative thinking, not pure concept.
5. **Risks & constraints** — regulatory considerations, operational challenges, model/technology limitations.
6. **Deliverable** — presentation in the final week as a structured, investment-quality proposal.

**Evaluation focus:** importance of the problem, quality/originality of the idea, soundness of the business model, depth of analytical thinking, feasibility of implementation. Projects must remain practical, focused, grounded in real-world constraints — both business insight and analytical rigor.

## User's Additional Goal (Jun Hao)
- Develop the idea into an **MVP / workable prototype** deployable to a web server with a fully developed frontend.
- Assess what is **lacking** that would hinder building a full prototype.
- Propose a **sound, revenue-generating business model** that makes the idea a viable business.
- Research **risks, constraints, regulatory and operational challenges, and limitations**.

## Source Pitch — "Before You Sign" (Ivory Lim)
**Tagline:** Helping consumers understand the real cost and consequence *before* buying financial products.

**Problem framing:** Consumers see the product pitch before they see the full consequence. Risk, fees, and suitability are unclear until *after* the purchase decision. Pitch phrases that obscure consequence: "safe growth", "7% returns a year", "monthly income", "balanced fund", "suitable for your profile".

**Core insight — risk needs personal context (worked example):**
- Person A: S$125k liquid savings; a S$100k product = 80% of liquid wealth → a 20% fall is a serious liquidity event.
- Person B: S$1m liquid savings; the same S$100k = 10% → a 20% fall is more manageable.
- "Safe growth" is too vague (capital preservation? low volatility? income? FD-plus? long-term growth?). Capital guarantee should never be assumed — users should ask *where the document states the guarantee and who provides it*.

**Proposed product — a "Before You Sign" report, five modules:**
1. **Product Scan** — reads the product summary, flags key terms.
2. **Fee Lens** — shows full cost impact before purchase.
3. **RiskFit Report** — tests suitability against the user's profile.
4. **Decision Gap Checklist** — highlights missing information and open questions.
5. **Portfolio Mirror** — shows how this decision changes the overall portfolio.

**Key question the product answers:** "What happens if I buy this?"

**Monetisation (as pitched):**
1. First full report after sign-up (one complete report on a real product the user is considering).
2. Basic free scan (product summary, guarantee check, count of concern areas).
3. Paid full report (fee breakdown, dollar impact, downside scenarios, contextual fit, adviser questions).
4. Premium access (multiple reports, saved reviews, Portfolio Mirror, portfolio impact simulation).
5. Family access (review products for parents, spouse, household planning).
